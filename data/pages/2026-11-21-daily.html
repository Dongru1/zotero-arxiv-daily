Zeyuan Li · Relevance 6.3

We present DRVEXO-4B-Unified, a modular multimodal assistant built around a 4B language backbone. A self-contained checkpoint combines text, image, and speech inputs with reference-conditioned speech output and structured interaction. TLDR: DRVEXO-4B-Unified is a modular 4B-backbone multimodal assistant that unifies text, image, and speech understanding with reference-conditioned speech synthesis and task-conditioned candidate scoring, achieving strong MMMU, IFEval, and MATH-500 results plus low-latency speech generation without test-time scaling.

Joint Language Modeling for In-the-Wild Speech-Scene Generation with Distinct Token Representations

Semin Kim, Ju Yeon Kang, Sunghwan Ahn, Seonuk Kim, Nam Soo Kim · Relevance 6.2

Speech-scene generation aims to synthesize intelligible target speech together with diverse environmental sounds as a coherent acoustic scene. The task is considered challenging in two respects. Speech and environmental audio differ substantially in their semantic and acoustic characteristics, yet existing approaches typically model the complete scene using a single mixed acoustic representation. At the same time, the two sounds are closely related in real-world speech scenes, whereas conventional approaches usually rely on simulated scenes mixed from independently recorded sources. We propose a large language model (LLM)-based framework for joint modeling of distinct speech and environment token streams. A source-aware neural audio codec extracts temporally aligned speech and environment tokens from mixed audio, and a pretrained LLM generates both token streams in an interleaved fashion conditioned on the natural-language scene description and speech content. This formulation preserves source-specific representations while modeling the token streams within a shared generative context. Since both streams are extracted directly from mixed recordings, the model can also be adapted to in-the-wild speech scenes without isolated sources. Our experimental results demonstrate that the proposed framework improves speech intelligibility, scene relevance, and audio quality over existing speech-scene generation techniques, with consistent gains from real-world adaptation. We further investigate the effects of distinct token representations and interleaved generation, and analyze whether the environmental sound remains stable while speech is active and how the two streams interact.

TLDR: This paper proposes an LLM-based framework that jointly models distinct, temporally aligned speech and environmental-sound token streams extracted from mixed audio by a source-aware neural codec, generating them interleaved from scene descriptions and speech content and enabling adaptation to in-the-wild speech scenes.

We present Xing-Audio-NVVASR, an end-to-end recognizer trained through progressive data mining and iterative supervision refinement. Guided by unified acoustic criteria, two heterogeneous models cross-audit annotations, flagging disagreements for teacher reannotation. We plan to release the model weights, inference code, four evaluation benchmarks, annotation guidelines, and evaluation code.

TLDR: Xing-Audio-NVVASR tackles scarce and unreliable non-verbal vocalization (NVV) supervision by progressively mining roughly 800K utterances and iteratively refining six-class NVV labels through cross-model auditing, teacher reannotation, and breath screening, training a single 1.7B-parameter end-to-end recognizer that jointly transcribes speech and inline NVV tags and ranks first among nine models on four human-annotated benchmarks.

Nathaniel Cohen, Nicolas Dufour, Am\'elie Royer, Alasdair Newson, Patrick P\'erez · Relevance 5.6

Current audio-driven video diffusion models can synthesize realistic talking-head videos but generally require dozens of diffusion denoising steps over multi-second video chunks, making them unsuitable for streaming applications such as voice assistants and virtual avatars, where low-latency and online causal generation are required properties. AURA generates video chunk by chunk with a rolling KV cache, requiring only the audio of the current chunk to start generating, rather than a multi-second audio look-ahead like bidirectional models. TLDR: AURA is a two-stage-distilled causal audio-driven talking-head video model that generates chunk-by-chunk with a rolling KV cache, enabling real-time streaming avatars on a single GPU—576p at 20.7 fps with AURA 1.3B and 720p at 9.7 fps with AURA 5B—while matching or approaching its bidirectional teacher’s quality.

UMelCodec: An Ultra-Low-Bitrate and Lightweight Mel-Domain Neural Speech Codec

Hui-Peng Du, Yang Ai, Zhen-Hua Ling · Relevance 5.1

Ultra-low-bitrate speech coding is crucial for bandwidth-constrained communication and compact storage, but preserving intelligibility, naturalness, and fine-grained acoustic details at bitrates of only a few hundred bps remains challenging. Existing purely acoustic codecs generally operate at relatively high bitrates, whereas incorporating semantic representations can reduce the bitrate, but at the cost of increased complexity. Therefore, we propose UMelCodec, a purely acoustic neural speech codec operating in the mel domain at an ultra-low bitrate of 325 bps with a lightweight architecture. UMelCodec consists of two stages. In the first mel-domain coding stage, alias-free Snake-ConvNeXt (AF-SC) encoder-decoder backbones model temporal and harmonic structures, a factorized single-codebook vector quantizer (VQ) enables ultra-low-bitrate quantization, and a multi-scale mel-spectrogram discriminator (MSMD) improves mel-spectrogram reconstruction. In the second waveform synthesis stage, a lightweight modified discrete cosine transform (MDCT)-based neural vocoder predicts the MDCT spectrum from the reconstructed mel spectrogram and recovers the waveform through inverse MDCT. Experiments on LibriTTS dataset demonstrate that UMelCodec outperforms strong baselines across most objective reconstruction metrics while maintaining competitive subjective naturalness. In terms of model complexity and inference efficiency, UMelCodec has only 19.67 M parameters, requires 1.93 G MACs, and supports real-time inference on both GPU and CPU platforms.

TLDR: UMelCodec is a lightweight, purely acoustic two-stage mel-domain neural speech codec that combines AF-SC encoder–decoder backbones, factorized single-codebook VQ, and an MDCT-based vocoder to achieve ultra-low-bitrate 325 bps coding with competitive reconstruction quality and real-time inference.

When Sounds Turn Heads: Recovering Event Timing That Audio LLMs Fail to Say

Jongbhin Woo, Jaewook Jeong, Joon Son Chung · Relevance 5.0

Audio LLMs can know when a queried sound occurs but fail to say it. We find a few attention heads that track the queried sound: they shift when the query changes, follow the sound when it moves, and removing or blocking them often hurts the model's timestamps. Yet the answer does not express what they track. Intervals read directly from these heads outperform the model's original answer and recover events that its timestamps miss. To bring this timing into the answer, we introduce head-guided decoding, which needs no training. It keeps the model's decision on whether the sound is present and rescores its candidate timestamps with a prior from these heads. Heads selected on development data transfer unchanged to new recordings and free-text queries, where head-guided decoding improves the timestamps of three audio LLMs, scores above their head readout on every benchmark, and, in most settings, outperforms random-head guidance. On recordings released after the models were trained, head-guided decoding raises mIoU from 0.07-0.36 to 0.48-0.56. These results suggest that poor temporal answers in audio LLMs partly reflect a failure to say rather than to hear, and that the model's own attention can close part of this gap.

TLDR: Audio LLMs internally encode queried sound timing in a few attention heads that their answers fail to express, and training-free head-guided decoding leverages this hidden timing to improve event timestamp recovery across models and benchmarks.

Aarne Talman, Stergios Chatzikyriakidis · Relevance 4.7

Prosodic prominence can largely be predicted from the words of a sentence, but part of it depends on how the speaker reads it. We study how much of prominence language models recover from text, whether they can report it when asked, and what the speech signal adds. On read English speech labelled for word-level prominence, we compare frozen and fine-tuned text models, prompted large language models (LLMs) and speech models on the same words. TLDR: Language models encode more information about prosodic prominence than they can explicitly report—prompting fails to elicit it and text only partially determines prominence—whereas speech models substantially outperform them by using acoustic cues.

Existing training-free methods globally adjust the effect of dialogue history on generation, making it difficult to suppress misleading information without weakening useful context. To address this trade-off, we introduce Support-Guided Triadic Decoding (SGTD), a training-free framework that selectively controls the influence of unreliable history and fuses complementary predictions during decoding. TLDR: **TLDR:** The paper proposes Support-Guided Triadic Decoding (SGTD), a training-free framework that estimates visual support for historical answers, selectively downweights unreliable history via targeted attention intervention, and fuses support-refined, full-history, and answer-free visual predictions with a plausibility constraint to mitigate hallucination propagation in multimodal multi-turn dialogue with LVLMs.

Humanoid agents in everyday environments are expected to react to diverse cues as humans do, with semantically appropriate and physically executable behaviors. Existing cue-driven reaction generation methods support only a narrow range of cue modalities and are limited to purely kinematic generation, leaving the resulting reactions susceptible to physical artifacts. To this end, we present MixReact, the first unified framework for physics-based reactive humanoid control from text, audio, and video cues, integrating a Discrete--Continuous Mixture of Transformers (DisCo-MoT) for state prediction with a History-aware Action Transformer (HAT) for physical execution. DisCo-MoT couples a discrete masked-modeling branch with a continuous autoregressive diffusion branch via one-way mixture attention within a MoT architecture. Guidance from discrete context mitigates collapse into averaged behaviors in one-to-many reaction generation, while direct prediction in continuous state space bypasses the decoding bottleneck of discrete representations. HAT then maps the predicted next state and observed execution history to executable actions, enabling robust closed-loop control in physics-based simulation. Experiments across all three modalities demonstrate consistent improvements over the baselines in fidelity, diversity, and physical consistency, while ablations validate the complementary contributions of the components. Our code will be released to facilitate future research. Project page: https://binlee26.github.io/MixReact_Page/

TLDR: **TLDR:** MixReact is a unified framework that uses a Discrete–Continuous Mixture of Transformers to generate diverse, semantically appropriate humanoid reactions from text, audio, or video cues and a History-aware Action Transformer to execute them as physically plausible, closed-loop actions in simulation.

NariQ: Sub-3-Bit Quantization for Long-Horizon Agentic Tasks

Doyeob Kim · Relevance 4.1

Long-horizon agentic tasks require a model to call tools over many steps at contexts of 100K tokens or more, yet many low-bit quantization methods are calibrated on short web text and judged on perplexity. This mismatch is costly: existing quantizations of Qwen3.8-27B at or below 3 bits per weight match BF16 on shorter tasks like AIME, yet in published results solve at most 72% as many tasks as BF16 on DeepSWE, a long-horizon agentic benchmark. We propose NariQ, a trellis-coded post-training quantization method that calibrates on tokens generated by the BF16 model in agent sessions. On Qwen3.8-27B, NariQ at 3.0 and 2.5 bits per weight stays level with BF16 on two long-horizon agentic benchmarks, DeepSWE and Terminal Bench, as well as on AIME and RealWorldQA. Our checkpoint at 2.0 bits per weight trails this performance. To our knowledge, these are the first quantizations at or below 3 bits per weight shown to match their full-precision version on long-horizon agentic tasks. We release the files as Qwen3.8-27B-NariQ-GGUF (https://huggingface.co/nari-labs/Qwen3.8-27B-NariQ-GGUF), with inference available through our llama.cpp fork at https://github.com/nari-labs/nari-llama.cpp.

TLDR: NariQ is a trellis-coded post-training quantization method calibrated on BF16-generated agent-session tokens that, at 2.5–3.0 bits per weight, matches BF16 on long-horizon agentic benchmarks such as DeepSWE and Terminal Bench, making it the first sub-3-bit quantization shown to preserve full-precision agentic performance.

CANDOR: Contrastive Self-Denoising Rewards for Label-Free Text-to-Image Post-Training

Junhyeok Lee, Byung-Hoon Kim, Kyu Sung Choi · Relevance 4.1

Post-training text-to-image generators with reinforcement learning usually relies on external reward models that need annotated preferences or auxiliary evaluators and are prone to reward hacking. We show that the generator's own denoising accuracy, used by a recent intrinsic reward, is a poor proxy for prompt fidelity. Within a same-prompt group, this absolute self-confidence is dominated by image complexity and ranks correct samples above incorrect ones near chance. We introduce CANDOR (Contrastive Alignment from Null-referenced Denoising lOg-Ratio), the log-ratio of unconditional to conditional denoising errors under shared noise probes, which measures how much the prompt helps the model denoise its own sample. It cancels prompt-independent difficulty that scales both errors and ranks the better of two same-prompt samples higher in 60% and 63% of pairs on compositional generation and text rendering. As the only Flow-GRPO reward for a flow-matching model, CANDOR improves text rendering, aesthetic and human-preference scores over the base model and a prior absolute self-confidence reward, with text-rendering and preference gains that stronger classifier-free guidance does not reproduce. On public Flow-GRPO adapters trained on external rewards, CANDOR further raises PickScore and ImageReward, so it can complement those rewards. Code is available at https://github.com/jhlee0619/candor.

TLDR: CANDOR introduces a label-free intrinsic reward for text-to-image post-training that uses the log-ratio of unconditional to conditional denoising errors to measure how much the prompt improves denoising, avoiding external reward models and the image-complexity confound of absolute self-confidence while improving text rendering, aesthetics, and human preference.

Vision-language models (VLMs) integrate visual and textual information, but their outputs do not reveal internal concept representations. We introduce the Multimodal Concept Lens (MC-Lens), a multimodal Jacobian lens, to study these functions in VLM concept processing. Its vision encoder and language decoder lenses map intermediate states to a shared semantic space defined by the decoder's output vocabulary to compare concept accessibility across modalities. MC-Lens supports internal concept interventions to examine the role of accessed concepts in downstream computation. (i) Accessibility and selective access. TLDR: The paper introduces MC-Lens, a multimodal Jacobian lens that maps vision-encoder and language-decoder states into a shared vocabulary-defined semantic space and uses counterfactual interventions to show that VLM concept representations are selectively accessible, causally used in downstream reasoning, and exhibit shared global-workspace-like patterns across model families and sizes.

FrontierReplay: Split-Free Exact Prefix Caching for Hybrid Language Models

Zekun Wang, Yizhuo Wang · Relevance 4.1

Hybrid language models combine self-attention with recurrent layers whose in-place state updates restrict prefix reuse to exact saved positions. Saving states on a coarse grid wastes reusable prefixes, whereas existing fine-grained exact caches must cut the prefill forward pass at each mid-prompt checkpoint, adding scheduler steps that outweigh the reuse gains under load. We observe that across multi-turn chat, agent loops, document question answering, and tree search, prefix reuse almost always ends at structural boundaries predictable in advance, such as turn ends and known branch points. We present FrontierReplay, a prefix caching system that materializes exact checkpoints at these boundaries without cutting the prefill. While the forward pass runs uncut, FrontierReplay logs a short window of recurrent-layer inputs and invokes a cross-layer, state-only replay kernel to build the target state 6-37x cheaper per token than full recomputation without host synchronization. A frontier-aware retention policy prioritizes active anchors and proven branches while lending dead checkpoint slots to the attention KV cache. Across diverse hybrid architectures, model scales, and workloads, FrontierReplay delivers clear gains over existing exact caches, lowering median time to first token (TTFT) by 16-89% (up to 8.9x), raising 80% service-level objective (SLO) capacity by up to 1.73x, and improving goodput by up to 4.26x (3.67x at larger scales). Compared with approximate replay methods, FrontierReplay delivers up to 1.54x higher goodput while preserving exact task accuracy, avoiding the up to 17% accuracy drop suffered by approximation on long contexts.

TLDR: FrontierReplay is a split-free exact prefix-caching system for hybrid language models that places exact recurrent-state checkpoints at predictable structural prefix-reuse boundaries without cutting the prefill, using short input logging and state-only replay to rebuild checkpoints cheaply, thereby cutting TTFT by 16–89% and improving goodput by up to 4.26× over existing exact caches.

Further analyses examine PRM adaptation, multilingual reasoning quality, and the contributions of individual training components.

TLDR: PAIR-MED is a policy–reward alternating training framework that iteratively adapts a multilingual medical process reward model to the evolving reasoning policy and uses its step-level rewards to improve medical reasoning across 12 languages.

VisAlloc: Allocating Visual Supervision for On-Policy Self-Distillation

On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) offers a promising approach to improving visual reasoning in multimodal large language models (MLLMs). However, not all samples benefit equally from privileged visual guidance, and uniform token supervision can dilute visually grounded signals. We propose VisAlloc, a dual-level framework combining sample-level visual filtering with token-level counterfactual reweighting. A fixed paired-teacher filter retains samples answered correctly from an evidence-centered positive crop but incorrectly from a spatially displaced negative crop. At the token level, both EMA teachers score the same student-generated response, and their absolute log-probability differences measure view sensitivity. Additional supervision targets tokens admitted by sensitivity or teacher-distribution disagreement, with shared frozen-prior, template-bigram, and student--teacher divergence gates. Inverse-frequency redistribution preserves the total additional weight within each micro-batch, while the positive teacher remains the sole distillation target. Inference requires only the full-image student. Across eight benchmarks, our 4B and 9B models achieve macro-averages of 81.44 and 84.92, outperforming Vision-OPD reproductions by 0.97 and 0.52 points, respectively. Code on GitHub: https://github.com/Kaelyn01/VisAlloc

TLDR: VisAlloc improves visual reasoning in multimodal LLMs by filtering on-policy self-distillation samples with paired positive/negative visual crops and reweighting tokens via counterfactual teacher-view sensitivity, concentrating supervision on visually grounded signals.
