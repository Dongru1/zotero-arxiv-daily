Factorized Self-Supervised Speech Tokenization

Benjamin Van Niekerk, Jean-Philippe Letendre, Nicol Visser, Hugo Seute, Herman Kamper, Mirco Ravanelli · Relevance 6.3

Speech tokenizers convert audio into discrete units, enabling language models to process and generate speech. The goal is to learn compact, text-like representations while preserving enough detail to reconstruct a speaker's voice and delivery. We propose Facto: a factorized tokenizer that disentangles time-varying information (content and prosody) from static components (speaker identity and recording conditions). We build on a recent linear factorization method for self-supervised features, extending it to handle unseen speakers. Then, we cluster the factorized space and train a lightweight decoder to reconstruct audio from the resulting tokens. By removing the static components before clustering, our tokens are more robust to small noise perturbations. We validate this by testing token stability under various noise conditions. Next, we theoretically analyze Facto's convergence and scaling properties, quantifying how the factorization improves with the number of training speakers. Finally, we evaluate Facto on a range of downstream tasks. We show that greater token stability translates into improved text-to-speech performance and competitive results in voice conversion and spoken language modeling. Code and checkpoints at https://github.com/ubisoft/ubisoft-laforge-facto/ and demo page at https://ubisoft-laforge.github.io/speech/facto/.

TLDR: Facto is a factorized self-supervised speech tokenizer that disentangles time-varying content and prosody from static speaker and recording factors, clusters the content representation to produce more noise-robust tokens, and uses a lightweight decoder to improve text-to-speech while achieving competitive voice conversion and spoken language modeling.

OmniTag-LLM: Multilingual Multitask Speech Tagging

Aref Farhadipour, Francesco Verdini, Antonis Asonitis, Pierre-Edouard Honnet, Marzieh Razavi, Vijeta Avijeet, Juan Pablo Zuluaga Gomez · Relevance 6.1

Multimodal LLMs promise to replace the traditional cascade of specialist speech models with a single system that, from one audio clip, jointly transcribes and labels emotion, gender, language, and named entities. Yet most benchmarks still score these tasks in isolation, and largely in English. We introduce OmniTag-Bench, a five-language benchmark (en/de/es/fr/it) and training recipe for joint understanding: one audio clip in, one JSON object out (transcript, emotion, gender, LID, token-level NER). We evaluate ten open multimodal LLMs and a LoRA fine-tuned Qwen3-Omni on a unified prompt and scorer. Beyond aggregate scores, OmniTag-Bench reveals a systematic failure mode: several strong models often transcribe or translate non-English speech into English, inflating multilingual WER. Fine-tuning improves the base model on NER, emotion, and WER while nearly doubling accuracy on independent human-labeled emotion corpora, suggesting transferable gains beyond pseudo-label imitation. The fine-tuned model also generalizes to unseen but related languages, transferring to held-out Dutch. We release prompts, the scorer, and the labeling pipeline.

TLDR: OmniTag-LLM introduces a five-language benchmark and LoRA fine-tuned Qwen3-Omni model for jointly producing transcript, emotion, gender, language ID, and NER from a single audio clip, revealing a common “transcribe-into-English” failure in multilingual MLLMs while showing that fine-tuning improves multitask performance and transfers to unseen Dutch.

Jun Dong, Ronghui Li, Zhongyuan Hu, Yunqi Zhao, Lijie Guo, Ruizhang Xu, Di Kang, Linchao Bao, Xiu Li · Relevance 6.1

An embodied speaker's expressiveness depends not only on what is said, but also on how the voice sounds and how the body moves. However, existing joint speech-motion generation models are largely driven implicitly by transcript semantics or intermediate speech representations, offering little explicit control over motion hierarchy, body parts, or vocal style. Beyond Controllability: Speaker Diversity in Instruction-Guided Text-to-Speech

Yi-Cheng Lin, Jia-Kai Dong, Kuan-Yu Chen, Szu-Chi Chen, Jian-Jiun Ding, Sung-Feng Huang, Yusuke Hirota, Hung-yi Lee · Relevance 5.9

Instruction-guided text-to-speech (ITTS) lets users specify voice properties in natural language. Applications from conversational AI character voices to synthetic training data for downstream speech models depend on the system producing many distinct voices when the same broad category is requested repeatedly. No prior work audits whether ITTS outputs are in fact diverse, either compared to real speech or across attribute values. We evaluate seven open-source ITTS systems using speaker embeddings whose representational capacity we independently validate. Every system produces speaker distributions markedly narrower than real speech. Furthermore, female prompts produce less diverse voice pools than male prompts across most systems. Our code and data are available at https://github.com/dlion168/ITTS-Speaker-Diversity.

TLDR: An audit of seven open-source instruction-guided TTS systems finds that all produce speaker distributions much narrower than real speech—especially for female prompts—while accent and emotion prompts reveal controllability biases, showing that speaker diversity and controllability are complementary and must be evaluated together.

Jing Pan, Ruizhi Li, Ju Lin, Jing Zheng, Florian Metze · Relevance 5.8

LLM-based architectures offer strong generalization and instruction-following capabilities for streaming automatic speech recognition (ASR), but multi-talker use introduces coupled transcription, attribution, and latency challenges. Our wearable setting requires real-time transcription of a conversation between a near-field device wearer and a far-field partner. Because partial hypotheses are rendered on persistent sides of a dialogue UI, speaker roles cannot be permuted and each speaker tag must precede its turn. TLDR: The paper post-trains a streaming LLM-based multi-talker ASR model with reinforcement learning using verifiable fixed-role speaker-attributed WER and onset-delay rewards, jointly reducing SA-WER from 20.51% to 12.70% and cutting median onset latency by 65% for real-time wearable conversation transcription.

Decode Early, Verify Late: Streaming Offline Speech Recognizers Without Accuracy Loss

Doyeob Kim · Relevance 5.8

Voice agents need accurate transcripts soon after a user stops speaking. We propose Decode Early, Verify Late (DEVL), a training-free framework for streaming inference with offline ASR models. The server keeps partial transcripts as drafts and verifies the latest against the complete audio in one forward pass at turn end. Under stated input and decoding conditions, the final equals the model's greedy offline output in exact arithmetic. We adapt DEVL to four model families. In our five-recognizer comparison, 95-100% of final transcripts are byte-identical to the offline transcripts, WER stays within 0.13 points of offline, and single-stream time to final falls 1.5-7.3x. Serving Qwen3-ASR-1.7B on one A100, DEVL returns finals a median 22-26 ms after turn end at up to 64 streams, 2.7-3.9x sooner than the official streaming mode. It is the only system we tested that meets a 100 ms p99 target, up to 80 streams on Pipecat-STT and 64 on WildASR.

TLDR: DEVL is a training-free framework that streams offline ASR models by decoding draft partials early and verifying them against the full audio at turn end, achieving near-offline transcription accuracy with substantially faster final transcripts.

TLDR: This paper proposes a three-stage post-training framework—DPO cold start, multi-objective GSPO, and context-conditioned OPSD—to enable zero-shot voice cloning models to preserve speaker identity while adapting prosody expressively to the target text, substantially improving emotion and paralinguistic performance.

VoxMel TTS Technical Report

Wenhao Guan, Jiaxin Ye, Haitao Li, Yifan Duan, Junxi Liu, Wangjin Zhou, Lin Li, Qingyang Hong, Xie Chen · Relevance 5.7

Continuous autoregressive TTS relies on an acoustic target that enables stable next-latent modeling while preserving fine-grained acoustic details. While existing approaches employ learned compressed representations (e.g., continuous VAE latents) to render prediction tractable, they rely on complex neural encoders that often cap generation fidelity. Conversely, training-free targets like mel-spectrograms eliminate encoder overhead, yet their potential for autoregressive generation remains largely underexplored. In this work, we propose VoxMel, a tokenizer-free framework for autoregressive TTS that makes stable mel modeling tractable through a fixed, exactly invertible two-axis Haar transform. The transform first decomposes each mel spectrogram along frequency into local averages and a frequency residual, then splits the averaged component along time into Coarse-Mel and a temporal residual. A language-model backbone with a flow-matching head autoregressively generates Coarse-Mel. Non-autoregressive residual tiers then predict the temporal residual from Coarse-Mel, followed by the frequency residual conditioned on the reconstructed Frequency-Averaged Mel. Neither residual is fed back into the autoregressive history. Trained on 100K hours of Emilia, the supervised 0.6B model obtains 1.51\% WER and 0.738 speaker similarity (SIM) on test-en, and 1.29\% CER and 0.755 SIM on test-zh. GRPO improves these results to 1.15\% WER / 0.753 SIM and 1.17\% CER / 0.776 SIM, respectively. We also distill the guided flow head into two- and four-step students, requiring 2 or 4 head evaluations per coarse frame. The four-step student retains competitive SIM and nearly matches the supervised model in WER/CER.

TLDR: VoxMel is a tokenizer-free autoregressive TTS framework that uses a fixed, exactly invertible two-axis Haar transform to autoregressively generate only a coarse mel-spectrogram while non-autoregressive residual tiers reconstruct fine details, achieving strong zero-shot results such as 1.15% WER/0.753 SIM on test-en and 1.17% CER/0.776 SIM on test-zh after GRPO.

We test it with controlled interventions on a 0.6B model; all three fail: (i) phoneme-interleaved continued pretraining yields no spokenness gain under text-matched controls while raising repetition (+0.13 [+0.03, +0.23]); (ii) prosody-plan conditional generation reaches an adherence of only 0.257 against a pre-registered 0.50 gate; (iii) a rule-based reward built on TTS prosody-feature distance is imperceptible in double-blind evaluation by both humans (8/16) and a calibrated LLM judge (9/16). TLDR: The paper shows that injecting speech or prosody signals does not reduce Chinese “AI flavor” in a 0.6B model, traces the failure partly to weak evaluation, and introduces a calibrated LLM-judge protocol whose agree labels, used directly for SFT-anchored DPO, yield a held-out blind win rate of 0.833 and eliminate repetition without harming MMLU.
