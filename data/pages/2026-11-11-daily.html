M5-TTS: A Mini-Scale Multilingual and Multi-Dialect Text-to-Speech Language Model with Multi-Token Prediction

Wenhao Li, Jingbin Hu, Shengming Ling, Xiaming Ren, Haoyu Zhang, Jinrui Liang, Hanke Xie, Qirui Zhan, Guobin Ma, Huakang Chen, Chunbo Hao, Jianguo Zheng, Wenkang Hou, Dake Guo, Yuepeng Jiang, Linhan Ma, Yunxiang Chen, Houdun Liu, Su Feng, Chuang Ding, Pengcheng Zhu, Liumeng Xue, Lei Xie · Relevance 6.4

We present M5-TTS, a compact text-to-speech system supporting multilingual and Chinese multidialect synthesis, zero-shot voice cloning, speaker-specific multilingual generation, and CPU streaming. Its 50M-parameter language model, excluding the speech codec, combines a temporal backbone with a local multi-token prediction module over 16 codebooks. GRPO post-training combines global speaker-similarity and pause feedback with a token-level CER process advantage. Character-level error comparisons provide local feedback, with negative credit propagated backward within a clause and projected onto audio-token positions. M5-TTS achieves 1.37% CER on Seed-ZH and 2.91% WER on Seed-EN, corresponding to relative error reductions of 62.2% and 35.3% over MOSS-TTS-Nano with half the language-model parameters. It also achieves lower content error rates in five of six evaluated MiniMax languages, while difficult Chinese inputs remain a limitation. On four Intel Xeon Gold 6530 cores, it achieves a real-time factor of 0.653 and a warm first-chunk latency of 225ms. A 10-listener evaluation gives M5-TTS higher speaker-similarity and intelligibility ratings than MOSS-TTS-Nano, with a lower naturalness rating. We will release training, post-training, and inference code, together with voice-cloning and speaker-specific checkpoints, to support reproducible research on compact speech generation for on-device voice interaction.

TLDR: M5-TTS is a compact 50M-parameter multilingual and Chinese multi-dialect TTS language model that combines multi-token prediction over 16 codebooks with GRPO post-training using speaker-similarity, pause, and token-level CER feedback to achieve strong zero-shot voice cloning, low content error rates, and efficient CPU streaming.

WaveGen: Truly End-to-End Waveform Generation via Internal Spectral Trajectory Learning

Sang-Hoon Lee, Ha-Yeong Choi · Relevance 6.1

This work proposes WaveGen, a fully end-to-end diffusion-based text-to-waveform framework based on Spectral Forcing, which jointly learns an internal spectral trajectory and the target waveform trajectory within a single model, enabling direct end-to-end text-to-waveform generation. Unlike recent TTS pipelines, WaveGen does not rely on separately trained or externally decoded intermediate representations, such as neural audio codecs, Mel-spectrogram vocoders, or VAE-based latent models. In addition, WaveGen performs internal text-speech alignment within a single model, eliminating the need for external alignment modules such as duration predictors. To preserve a truly end-to-end formulation, our framework further avoids dependence on self-supervised representations, such as BERT-based text embeddings and Wav2Vec 2.0-based semantic representations, as well as semantic distillation methods for training optimization. To achieve this, we carefully design Spectral Forcing, a model architecture with disentangled diffusion heads, and a training objective based on a multi-scale spectral $v$-loss. We further validate the scalability of end-to-end TTS models by scaling the model size from 0.1B to 0.6B parameters. Using a single architecture, open-source data, and one-stage training, without external modules such as duration predictor or REPA, WaveGen demonstrates fully end-to-end waveform generation achieving state-of-the-art performance on the Emilia training dataset. In particular, WaveGen achieves a WER of 1.47 and a SIM of 0.75 on the Seed-en benchmark. Source code and checkpoints are available at https://github.com/sh-lee-prml/WaveGen

TLDR: WaveGen is a fully end-to-end diffusion-based text-to-waveform TTS framework that uses Spectral Forcing to jointly learn an internal spectral trajectory and the target waveform in a single model—without codecs, vocoders, latent models, duration predictors, or self-supervised representations—achieving state-of-the-art zero-shot performance with one-stage training.

REAL: Reward Modeling through Evidence-Linked Analysis of Lifelikeness in Generated Speech

Yaodong Song, Jie Lian, Xusheng Yang, Genliang Zhao, Hongjie Chen, Jian Kang, Zhen-Hua Ling, Jie Li, Yongxiang Li · Relevance 6.0

As modern text-to-speech systems become increasingly intelligible and natural, evaluation is shifting from detecting gross synthesis failures to identifying subtle departures from lifelike human delivery. Because large-scale listening tests are costly and slow, speech reward models can serve as scalable automatic judges for comparing generated utterances, curating data, and supporting iterative TTS development. Existing speech reward models can predict scores or pairwise preferences and may generate natural-language rationales, but their supervision is typically defined at the utterance or pair level. The connection between local acoustic phenomena and the final preference therefore remains implicit. We introduce REAL, a framework for Reward modeling through Evidence-linked Analysis of Lifelikeness in generated speech. Its central idea is to supervise not only the final pairwise preference but also a structured account of its supporting observations, linking local audio spans to perceptual phenomena, affected dimensions, and the overall judgment. Specifically, REAL-Jury constructs these evidence-linked reports through timestamp alignment, multi-model analysis, and dispute-oriented deliberation. REAL-Judge learns to generate them from paired audio inputs. REAL-Eval assesses preference prediction in two tiers, from salient perceptual differences to subtle distinctions among strong TTS systems. Instantiated with MiMo-Audio, REAL-Judge improves pairwise preference accuracy over its backbone by 16.01 and 11.41 percentage points on the two tiers, respectively, and outperforms the open-source speech reward models SpeechJudge and UniSRM on both tiers.

TLDR: The paper introduces REAL, a speech reward-modeling framework that trains a timestamp-aware judge on evidence-linked reports connecting local acoustic phenomena to perceptual dimensions and pairwise lifelikeness preferences, improving preference accuracy over prior open-source speech reward models.

Beyond the Transcript: Measuring and Improving the Use of Acoustic Information in Audio Language Models

Linkai Peng, Baorian Nuchged, Yuyang Yao · Relevance 5.7

Audio language model benchmarks provide comprehensive evaluation across diverse tasks. However, some tasks can be answered from the transcript or even the question alone, and benchmark scores can therefore confound acoustic understanding with language modeling ability. We distinguish lexical tasks, for which the transcript usually suffices, from global-attribute and within-recording-structure tasks, which require the model to analyse the recording beyond its words, either as a whole, for properties such as the speaker and emotion, or within it, for patterns such as stress and intonation. We evaluated 19 open models and Gemini 3.1 Pro on 92 tasks from 6 benchmarks and estimated the recording gain, the accuracy advantage of the original recording over references that retain its words, by comparing the recording with synthetic readings of its transcript and by adding it to the transcript. On attribute and structure tasks, the open models fall behind Gemini by 28.6 and 41.9 percentage points, and about half or more of each deficit lies in the recording gain. Nevertheless, linear probes in Gemma-4 12B and Qwen2.5-Omni-7B show that the acoustic information needed for answering is already readable in early language-model layers, while the readout of many cues declines in the layers that follow. We therefore constructed training data and trained LoRA adapters in these layers, with a content anchor, which raises attribute and structure accuracy by 12.8 to 44.4 points in the two models and keeps mean lexical accuracy within 1 point of the base model. Most of the improvement appears as larger recording gains. These results suggest that training improves the use of acoustic information that the models already encode.

TLDR: The paper shows that audio language models often fail to use acoustic information already encoded in early layers, and that targeted LoRA training in those layers can substantially improve non-lexical audio understanding without hurting lexical accuracy.

Textraction: Unified Text-Guided Speaker Extraction and Enhancement for Single- and Multi-Channel Audio

Aviad Eisenberg, Sharon Gannot, Shlomo E. Chazan · Relevance 5.7

We present Textraction, a text-guided speaker extraction framework that enables the selective extraction of desired speakers or noise sources from an audio mixture using only natural-language descriptions. The proposed method operates within a single unified model that supports both single-channel and multi-channel inputs and it allows conditioning on either semantic textual descriptions of the target source or on its spatial description. Dedicated single- and multi-channel encoders feed a shared stack of time--frequency transformer blocks, which is conditioned on the prompt and on the recording configuration in every block through cross-attention and estimates the target directly from the mixture in one forward pass, without a separation stage or a source-selection stage. The system can extract an arbitrary subset of entities from a mixture, assuming up to two simultaneously active speakers and two noise sources, and interprets prompts specifying which entities to keep, which to remove, or both. In addition, we show that the framework naturally applies to a speech-enhancement setting, in which a single target speaker is present alongside background noise. To circumvent the application of computationally costly large language models used in prior work, we introduce a lightweight, task-adapted text encoder obtained by contrastively fine-tuning a compact text-retrieval model, which learns to be sensitive to instruction polarity (e.g., keep vs. remove) while remaining robust to paraphrasing. Experiments across attribute-based extraction, spatially guided extraction, and speech enhancement show that the proposed approach effectively isolates the desired entities under various acoustic and spatial conditions, and matches or surpasses dedicated spatial-extraction and enhancement baselines while offering substantially greater flexibility.

TLDR: Textraction is a unified, lightweight text-guided framework that directly extracts or enhances target speakers and noise sources from single- or multi-channel audio mixtures in one forward pass using natural-language semantic or spatial prompts, matching or surpassing specialized baselines with greater flexibility.

TLDR: Fine-tuning Whisper with LoRA on a few hours of a dysarthric speaker’s own speech drastically reduces error rates and enables a locally run, real-time speech-to-synthesized-voice app that others can replicate as a DIY assistive technology.

Guidance at Pure Noise Erodes Speaker Similarity in Few-Step Token-Conditioned Flow-Matching TTS

Jaeho Lee, Han-Lim Choi · Relevance 5.5

Text-to-speech systems built from a language model and a flow-matching mel decoder now run the decoder with two to four Euler steps instead of the official ten or more. At two steps the word error rate (WER) is unchanged, but speaker similarity (SIM) drops by one to six points. This paper shows that most of this loss comes from classifier-free guidance on the first step. At pure noise the network can only return the conditional mean, so guidance shifts every trajectory by nearly the same vector. Later steps do not remove this shift, and its size grows with the length of the first step, so it is negligible with ten or more steps and accounts for most of the loss with two. Two training-free samplers are derived from this analysis. First-step unguided sampling (FSU) leaves the first step unguided and recovers $84$--$91\%$ of the two-step SIM loss on CosyVoice~2/3 with no significant WER increase. FSU-Mid replaces the two Euler steps by one midpoint step with an unguided first evaluation, recovers $77$--$85\%$ on IndexTTS-2 and matches the ten-step teacher on CosyVoice~2 and GLM-TTS. Both use three network function evaluations, one fewer than the official two-step sampler, and FSU-Mid gives the highest mean recovery among eight published guidance rules re-run at two steps on four decoders.

TLDR: The paper shows that applying classifier-free guidance at the first pure-noise step of few-step token-conditioned flow-matching TTS shifts all trajectories and causes most of the speaker-similarity loss, and it proposes training-free first-step-unguided samplers that recover most of the lost similarity without increasing WER.
