Gaspard: Expressive unified speech and gesture synthesis with continuous autoregressive flow-matching and decoupled classifier-free guidance

T\'eo Guichoux, Th\'eodor Lemerle, Catherine Pelachaud, Nicolas Obin · Relevance 5.7

Human communication involves highly coordinated multimodal signals such as speech and co-speech gestures. Existing approaches for generating both modalities commonly follow a cascaded pipeline, where speech is generated first and subsequently used to drive gesture generation; speech generation is therefore independent of gestures. We introduce Gaspard, a unified speech--gesture generation model based on continuous autoregressive generation with next-step flow-matching predictions, and trained on Seamless Interaction, a large-scale multimodal dataset. The heterogeneous temporal properties of speech and gesture motivate modality-specific prediction heads and decoupled classifier-free guidance over the two generation streams. This guidance rebalances the influence of speech and gesture at inference time, improving expressivity while mitigating dynamics collapse in continuous autoregressive gesture generation. Objective and perceptual evaluations show that Gaspard is a strong gesture generation model against unimodal baselines and that it produces highly synchronous speech and gestures compared to a matched cascaded system.

TLDR: Gaspard is a unified speech–gesture generation model that uses continuous autoregressive next-step flow-matching with modality-specific prediction heads and decoupled classifier-free guidance to produce more expressive, synchronous speech and co-speech gestures than cascaded baselines.

21Codec: Decoder-side Speaker Control for Text-to-Speech

David Lindevelt, Suzan Verberne, Joost Broekens · Relevance 5.5

We propose 21Codec, a streaming codec model with reduced recoverable speaker information from the token stream and decoder-side speaker control. Existing codec models infer speaker identity from token stream. Making them streamable is not sufficiently robust to speaker drift, because each chunk re-infers the identity from the local chunk context alone. Our adversarial speaker classifier and NANSY-style formant-shift data augmentation pushes the model to rely on the provided speaker embedding while reducing the recoverable speaker information from the token stream. We show that 21Codec can control speaker identity through the conditioning vector, and that the speaker identity is maintained across chunks during streaming. With the ability to control speaker identity even in mid-stream, 21Codec is a promising candidate for streaming TTS systems for better controllability of speaker identity.

TLDR: 21Codec is a streaming audio codec that removes recoverable speaker identity from its token stream and reintroduces controllable speaker identity at the decoder via speaker-embedding conditioning, enabling stable, mid-stream speaker control for text-to-speech.

Omni-modal large language models (omni-modal LLMs) can respond to spoken requests directly through voice and video calls. Such requests rely on the scene shared by the user and the assistant, so the same utterance may call for different help or for no response. Most audio-visual benchmarks provide questions as separate text input, and interactive benchmarks mostly score a response as a whole. It thus remains unclear whether a model recognizes when the user is asking for help, and whether its reply meets the user's need once the request is recognized. We propose O-IN-Bench to evaluate how well omni-modal LLMs understand spoken intentions. A model watches a complete video with native speech and outputs one text response, or [SILENCE] when no response is needed. O-IN-Bench contains 1,000 Chinese and English samples in five categories and 16 subcategories. When the speech is a conversation between peers rather than a request to the assistant, even strong models often keep responding. When a reply must satisfy several people, the best score is only 25.9. These results indicate that recognizing a request and meeting its requirements are separate abilities, and O-IN-Bench measures each of them.

TLDR: The paper introduces **O-IN-Bench**, a 1,000-sample Chinese/English audio-visual benchmark that evaluates whether omni-modal LLMs can recognize spoken intentions—including when to remain silent—and provide responses that actually meet the user’s need, finding that the best model scores only **59.6** and that recognizing a request does not guarantee satisfying it.

TLDR: This paper shows that SLAM-ASR speech recognition relies on induction heads in the frozen language model—along with paired heads that cancel attention noise—and that scaling these induction heads can improve transcription under noisy audio.

TLDR: TLDR: This position paper argues that agentic audio AI’s main bottleneck is now decision-making rather than listening, and shows that adding an explicit uncertainty-aware action-gating layer—deciding whether to act, wait, ask, or stay silent—substantially reduces unwanted actions while preserving most valid requests.

Why Multi-Source Audio Fails to Compose: A Geometric Diagnosis and Its Remedy

Anurag Singh, Prajwal Singh, Shanmuganathan Raman · Relevance 5.0

Multi-source audio-to-image generation exhibits consistent compositional failures when audio tokens occupy geometrically displaced, semantically unanchored positions in the CLIP text-token space, where the diffusion U-Net lacks the prior to reliably attend to them or ground them in distinct spatial regions. We formalize this as the geometric displacement phenomenon and expose it through a two-level clustering analysis comparing BEATs and CLAP embeddings at the raw encoder and projected CLIP token levels. Three geometric properties emerge as important: intra-class compactness, angular alignment with the CLIP text manifold, and inter-token spatial separation. We show that CLAP, whose contrastive language-audio pretraining aligns embeddings with natural language semantics, substantially outperforms BEATs on all three properties, improving the silhouette score by more than 3x and the Audio-Image Content score as well. Building on this analysis, we introduce a multi-token extension that requires no diffusion-backbone retraining and assigns each audio source to a distinct placeholder token in the CLIP tokenizer, complemented by a mixed inference-time objective that combines Attend-and-Excite with an A* spatial separation loss to resolve residual cross-token interference. For evaluation, we construct multi-audio compositional benchmarks from VGGSound (VGG2, VGG3, and VGGN) and extend six standard audio-image metrics to the multi-source setting. Across five benchmarks and four strong baselines, our method achieves strong results on the metrics most directly tied to compositional faithfulness, with AIC improvements of up to 2.25x over the strongest baseline and FID reduced by nearly 2x on two-source generation. We propose geometric displacement as a diagnostic framework for cross-modal generation systems that project learned embeddings into a pretrained contrastive space.

TLDR: The paper diagnoses multi-source audio-to-image compositional failures as geometric displacement of audio tokens in CLIP text-token space and remedies them with CLAP-based semantically aligned embeddings, per-source placeholder tokens, and a mixed Attend-and-Excite plus A* spatial-separation inference objective—without retraining the diffusion backbone—yielding stronger compositional faithfulness.

Autoregressive models generate tokens sequentially, while discrete diffusion models typically predict tokens independently within each denoising step. We propose ViLFlow (Vision-Language Flow), a continuous-flow vision-language model that combines joint modeling of output embeddings with parallel token decoding. ViLFlow adapts pretrained continuous language generation to visual question answering and image captioning through learned image guidance and prediction-aware token decoding. TLDR: ViLFlow is a continuous-flow vision-language model that jointly denoises token embeddings in parallel and uses teacher-distilled image guidance plus a prediction-aware token decoder to improve visual question answering and image captioning over autoregressive and masked-diffusion baselines.
