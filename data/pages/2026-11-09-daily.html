Benchmarking Speaker Embedders in the Sub-Second Regime for Real-Time Voice AI Agents
Abhishek Gautam
Unknown Affiliation
Relevance: 5.6
TLDR: Real-time Voice AI agents must separate the active user from intelligible background speech that survives conventional noise suppression. Background sources include television audio, family members, and colleagues. Production denoisers remove non-stationary environmental noise effectively. Background speech shares the spectral characteristics of the target and passes through, causing false acceptances in downstream speaker verification (SV) and automatic speech recognition (ASR). We benchmark open-source speaker embedders TitaNet-Large, TitaNet-Small, ECAPA-TDNN, CAM++, and SpeakerNet under a strict sub-second latency budget. Evaluation combines a controlled benchmark with a weighted human-annotated production set. Our controlled benchmark is built from real calls in which a second real speaker is injected into silence intervals at modulated amplitude. We report three findings. First, denoising before embedding is generally harmful and raises equal error rate (EER) across architectures. Second, truncating audio below the full voice-activity segment degrades every model. Third, roughly half of the TitaNet embedding is redundant for this task. Trailing dimensions encode population attributes such as language and gender. Such attributes carry little discriminative value in a demographically homogeneous user base. We distill these results into deployment guidance. Verification should run on raw audio with full voice-activity chunks. Thresholds should favor precision over F1. Truncated embeddings reduce the cost of similarity search.

Unknown Affiliation
Relevance: 5.3
TLDR: Automated red-teaming of text-to-audio models requires test prompts that pass textual screening and expose harmful acoustic content. Our key idea is to represent test descriptions as structured acoustic plans that organize foreground sounds, context, and vocal attributes. To this end, we propose acoustic plan learning, a framework that learns and refines these plans using waveform feedback. Shao-Chun Hu, Zi-Xiang Lin, Hsin-Min Wang, Jeih-Weih Hung, Hung-Shin Lee
Unknown Affiliation
Relevance: 5.1
TLDR: Low-complexity causal speech separation must preserve speaker information and allocate residual mixture energy without relying on future feature frames. We present GTCRN-SS, a two-stage time-frequency separator that adapts the compact GTCRN enhancement backbone to fixed-output speech separation. The model combines internal residual allocation, reconstruction-conditioned refinement, and frame-aligned recurrent-state transfer. Its complex-mask estimator assigns non-target content to an unreturned residual branch, while the complementary speech estimate conditions a second stage whose final masks remain anchored to the original mixture. These results establish GTCRN-SS as an effective quality-efficiency operating point for causal monaural speech separation.

Conflict-Aware Meta-Learning for Generalizable Speech Deepfake Detection
Siqing Qin, Kong Aik Lee, Youzhi TU, Eng Siong Chng, Man-Wai Mak
Unknown Affiliation
Relevance: 4.7
TLDR: Speech deepfake detectors built on large self-supervised learning front-ends achieve high in-domain accuracy, yet suffer degradation under domain shift caused by unseen synthesizers, transmission codecs, or unfamiliar acoustic conditions. Meta-learning, particularly model-agnostic meta-learning for domain generalization (MLDG), addresses this issue by simulating domain shifts. However, applying MLDG directly introduces a bi-level optimization challenge that remains unsolved. First, effective domain-shift emulation requires genuine distributional gaps between meta-splits, yet such gaps can frequently produce gradient conflicts, destabilizing the bi-level optimization. Second, the stochastic nature of mini-batch gradients causes the reference direction to fluctuate across iterations, resulting in an optimization trajectory that fails to achieve domain generalization. We propose a conflict-aware meta-learning (CAML) framework, which addresses both challenges through three complementary components. Firstly, base domain gradient surgery (base-DGS) introduces an asymmetric projection that treats the meta-train gradient as a primary anchor. Secondly, EMA-DGS replaces the unstable anchor with its exponential moving average (EMA), providing a stable optimization trajectory anchor that substantially reduces domain-shift-induced conflict detections. Thirdly, layer-wise DGS (LW-DGS) further extends the framework to individual parameter tensors, enabling fine-grained, layer-specific intervention that targets conflict-prone layers while leaving well-aligned parameters intact. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed CAML framework achieves lower EER than the compared baselines on most cross-dataset evaluations across multiple cross-dataset benchmarks and architectures, achieving a maximum relative improvement of 45.2% in terms of EER.
