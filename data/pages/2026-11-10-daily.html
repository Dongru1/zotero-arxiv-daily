Benchmarking Speaker Embedders in the Sub-Second Regime for Real-Time Voice AI Agents

Abhishek Gautam · Relevance 5.7

Real-time Voice AI agents must separate the active user from intelligible background speech that survives conventional noise suppression. Background sources include television audio, family members, and colleagues. Production denoisers remove non-stationary environmental noise effectively. Background speech shares the spectral characteristics of the target and passes through, causing false acceptances in downstream speaker verification (SV) and automatic speech recognition (ASR). We benchmark open-source speaker embedders TitaNet-Large, TitaNet-Small, ECAPA-TDNN, CAM++, and SpeakerNet under a strict sub-second latency budget. Evaluation combines a controlled benchmark with a weighted human-annotated production set. Our controlled benchmark is built from real calls in which a second real speaker is injected into silence intervals at modulated amplitude. We report three findings. First, denoising before embedding is generally harmful and raises equal error rate (EER) across architectures. Second, truncating audio below the full voice-activity segment degrades every model. Third, roughly half of the TitaNet embedding is redundant for this task. Trailing dimensions encode population attributes such as language and gender. Such attributes carry little discriminative value in a demographically homogeneous user base. We distill these results into deployment guidance. Verification should run on raw audio with full voice-activity chunks. Thresholds should favor precision over F1. Truncated embeddings reduce the cost of similarity search.

TLDR: This paper benchmarks five open-source speaker embedders under sub-second latency for real-time voice agents and finds that raw full voice-activity audio outperforms denoised or truncated inputs, while roughly half of TitaNet’s embedding dimensions are redundant, yielding deployment guidance that favors precision and cheaper similarity search.

Conflict-Aware Meta-Learning for Generalizable Speech Deepfake Detection

Siqing Qin, Kong Aik Lee, Youzhi TU, Eng Siong Chng, Man-Wai Mak · Relevance 4.8

Speech deepfake detectors built on large self-supervised learning front-ends achieve high in-domain accuracy, yet suffer degradation under domain shift caused by unseen synthesizers, transmission codecs, or unfamiliar acoustic conditions. Meta-learning, particularly model-agnostic meta-learning for domain generalization (MLDG), addresses this issue by simulating domain shifts. However, applying MLDG directly introduces a bi-level optimization challenge that remains unsolved. First, effective domain-shift emulation requires genuine distributional gaps between meta-splits, yet such gaps can frequently produce gradient conflicts, destabilizing the bi-level optimization. Second, the stochastic nature of mini-batch gradients causes the reference direction to fluctuate across iterations, resulting in an optimization trajectory that fails to achieve domain generalization. We propose a conflict-aware meta-learning (CAML) framework, which addresses both challenges through three complementary components. Firstly, base domain gradient surgery (base-DGS) introduces an asymmetric projection that treats the meta-train gradient as a primary anchor. Secondly, EMA-DGS replaces the unstable anchor with its exponential moving average (EMA), providing a stable optimization trajectory anchor that substantially reduces domain-shift-induced conflict detections. Thirdly, layer-wise DGS (LW-DGS) further extends the framework to individual parameter tensors, enabling fine-grained, layer-specific intervention that targets conflict-prone layers while leaving well-aligned parameters intact. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed CAML framework achieves lower EER than the compared baselines on most cross-dataset evaluations across multiple cross-dataset benchmarks and architectures, achieving a maximum relative improvement of 45.2% in terms of EER.

TLDR: The paper proposes a conflict-aware meta-learning (CAML) framework that stabilizes MLDG for generalizable speech deepfake detection by using asymmetric, EMA-anchored, and layer-wise domain gradient surgery to resolve meta-train/meta-test gradient conflicts, achieving lower EER on most cross-dataset benchmarks with up to 45.2% relative improvement.
